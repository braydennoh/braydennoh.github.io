Hi, I'm Brayden Noh, a PhD student in Earth and Planetary Sciences at Harvard University, where I am advised by Brendan Meade. My research focuses on earthquake cycles and the interactions between tectonic deformation and landscape evolution, combining physical process modeling with statistical approaches.

Brief bio

I completed my undergraduate studies at Caltech, where I worked with Michael Lamb, Omar Wani, Karl Lang, and Vinamra Agrawal on projects ranging from asteroid impact simulations to probabilistic geomorphic risk modeling.

At Caltech, I built Bayesian models and Monte Carlo simulations to predict river migration, delivering probabilistic hazard forecasts for infrastructure risk assessment. I also developed image analysis pipelines to infer 3D fractal dimensions of mud flocs from 2D imaging, improving sediment transport prediction in aquatic environments.

Previously, I was an NSF REU Researcher at Georgia Tech, developing UAV-based remote sensing and sediment transport modeling techniques for paleo-discharge estimation. Before that, I modeled asteroid atmospheric entry and crater formation at Auburn University, producing award-winning visualizations recognized by the 2021 AGU Michael H. Freilich Student Visualization Competition.
