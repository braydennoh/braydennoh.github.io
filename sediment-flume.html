Sediment Flume

Water flows over a granular bed in a periodic two-dimensional channel. The water is resolved by smoothed particle hydrodynamics (SPH) and the grains by the discrete element method (DEM). Tilting the channel increases the flow velocity and the shear stress the flow exerts on the bed, and obstacles can be drawn directly on the domain with the wall brush.

Three bed materials are available. Sand has grain diameters d of roughly 0.8 to 1.2 cm at a density ρs of 2650 kg/m³, gravel spans 1.4 to 2.2 cm at 2700 kg/m³, and boulder spans 2.3 to 3.8 cm at 2700 kg/m³. Switching material rebuilds the run. Coarser and denser grains weigh more per unit of exposed area and require a proportionally larger bed stress to move. Grain sizes are flume-scaled, and because two-dimensional discs interlock across the full channel width, motion onset sits well above the classical Shields curve. The model is a teaching instrument, not a rating curve.

Slope and water depth set the driving stress. For steady channel flow the bed shear stress is the depth–slope product τ = ρghS, so deepening the water or steepening the channel are two routes to the same end. The Shields number τ* = hS / [(ρs/ρ − 1) d] compares this stress with the immersed weight of a grain, and the bed mobilizes when τ* exceeds the critical value τ*c of Soulsby and Whitehouse (1997). Bed height sets the sediment inventory available for transport.

The eddy viscosity ν stands in for turbulence, which a laminar two-dimensional model cannot resolve. It controls how fast the equilibrium current runs at a given slope and how efficiently momentum is carried down to the bed. Lower ν lets the mid-flow race ahead of the near-bed layer while higher ν couples them and delivers more of the driving stress to the grains. The drag coefficient Cd scales both the entrainment force the current exerts on exposed grains and the resistance felt by grains settling or saltating through the water column, so raising it mobilizes the bed earlier and shortens saltation hops.

The GPU engine runs the physics with WebGPU at up to about 18,000 water particles while the CPU engine is the reference implementation restricted to the Lite preset. If the machine cannot sustain real time the simulation dilates time, shown as speed, rather than degrading the physics.
