Where are you?

A small demonstration of what a website can, and cannot, learn about where you are.

Your browser decides. It will ask before sharing anything, and if you say no, this page learns nothing new.

There are two very different kinds of location a website can work with.

The first is approximate. The moment you open any page, your device sends its IP address to the server so it knows where to send the page back. An IP address can be matched to a rough location, usually a city or region, sometimes only your internet provider. It is automatic, it needs no permission, and it is often wrong by many kilometers. This page does not do that, because it is a static site with nowhere to record it.

The second is precise. A browser can report your real position, sometimes to within a few meters, using GPS, nearby Wi-Fi, and cell towers. This is the kind that matters, and a website cannot take it quietly. Your browser has to ask you first, and you have to agree. That is the button below.

When you click it, your browser will show its own prompt, something like "braydennoh.github.io wants to know your location," with Allow and Block. Everything that comes back stays in this page, in your browser. It is not sent anywhere and it is not saved.

Try it

Nothing is transmitted or stored. Reload the page and it is gone.
