Meander Village

Settlements on meandering river floodplains face a fundamental tension: the river creates fertile land, but also erodes it away. As soil quality decays with time since the last channel visit, households must periodically relocate. This model explores how population density transforms displacement from local reshuffling into long-distance forced migration.

By Brayden Noh.

The floodplain is an Eulerian grid where each cell tracks when the river last occupied it. Soil quality decays exponentially:

\[ q(\mathbf{x}, t) = 0.5^{\,(t - t_{\text{last}}(\mathbf{x})) \,/\, T_{1/2}} \]

A household must relocate when \(q < 0.5\) or when the river erodes its cell. It searches outward in expanding Chebyshev rings (ring 1 = 8 neighbors, ring 2 = 16, …) until finding an unoccupied cell with \(q \geq 0.5\). The Chebyshev distance \(d = \max(|\Delta r|, |\Delta c|)\) measures displacement effort. Single occupancy is enforced.

At low density, most moves are ring 1 (local). As population approaches the carrying capacity (~2200 habitable cells at 50 m resolution), search rings explode and displacement becomes long-range. Press Play and adjust the population slider.
