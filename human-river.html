Human-River Geomorphic Coupling

Rivers draw people in — they provide water, food, and transport — but they also wander, eroding what is built beside them. This model turns that tension into a cellular automaton: a fixed population settles as close to a migrating river as it can, is forced to rebuild when the channel erodes its land, and moves voluntarily only when a distinctly better spot opens up.

The whole model has just three knobs: how fast the river migrates, how many people there are, and how reluctant they are to move.

By Brayden Noh.

The grid has two cell types: river cells \(R(\mathbf{x},t)=1\) (blue) and floodplain cells \(R=0\) (black). Floodplain cells can host settlement cells \(V(\mathbf{x},t)=1\), colored by their status each timestep: stable (orange — persisted from the previous step), new (green — just built), abandoned (white — voluntarily vacated), and drowned (red — destroyed by the advancing river). Population is conserved: \(\sum_{\mathbf{x}} V(\mathbf{x},t) = N\) for all \(t\).

Every household wants one thing: to be as close to the river as possible. So each floodplain cell has utility

\[ U(\mathbf{x}, t) \;=\; -\,\mathcal{D}(\mathbf{x}, t), \qquad \mathcal{D}(\mathbf{x},t) \;=\; \min_{\mathbf{y}:\,R(\mathbf{y},t)=1} \|\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{y}\|, \]

where \(\mathcal{D}\) is the distance (in cells) to the nearest river cell. There is no other term — no clustering bonus, no weights. Movement happens two ways:

  • Forced migration. When the river erodes a settlement cell, that household has no choice: it is relocated to the nearest open cell to the river.
  • Voluntary migration. The worst-placed household relocates to the best open cell only if that cell is at least \(\mu\) cells closer to the river, \[ \mathcal{D}_{\text{worst}} - \mathcal{D}_{\text{best}} \;>\; \mu , \] repeated until no such move remains. Here \(\mu\) is friction — reluctance to move.

Friction is the single behavioral parameter. At \(\mu = 0\) the rule is purely greedy: households relocate for any improvement, so the settlement always occupies the \(N\) cells closest to the river — a parameter-free model. Larger \(\mu\) makes the settlement sticky, so it lags behind the migrating channel and relocates in rarer, larger jumps.

The migration rate is simply the timestep: how many river frames the channel advances each step. A larger migration rate moves the river farther per step, so it drowns more land and forces more relocation. Together with the population \(N\) and the friction \(\mu\), that is the entire model.
