Meander Village

Rivers draw people in: water, food, transport. But rivers also wander, eroding what is built beside them. Here households chase a migrating river across its floodplain, drowning and rebuilding as the channel sweeps by.

The river follows the Howard-Knutson rule, where each centerline node migrates along its local normal at the rate

R = Ω klWκ + Γ ⟨klWκ⟩,     ⟨f ⟩ = Σup f e−αs ⁄ Σup e−αs

where κ is curvature, s is distance upstream, Ω = −1, Γ = 2.5, W = 100 m, and the memory length ℓ = 1/(αW) is the meander scale. When two banks come within the cutoff distance, the loop is cut off as an oxbow. Each step the channel is rasterized onto a grid, and a distance transform gives every floodplain cell its distance D to the river.

Households settle the grid with utility

U = −wd D + wc C

with wd = 1 and C the number of settled neighbors (cohesion). A household the river erodes is relocated to the best open cell: forced migration. The worst-placed household moves to the best open cell when it gains more than the stubbornness μ: voluntary migration. Population is conserved.

Meander Drift

By Brayden Noh and Omar Wani.

Meandering rivers migrate laterally across their floodplains, and communities that depend on the river are displaced in the process. The curvature-migration phase lag causes bends to sweep asymmetrically, producing a net along-valley drift of river-seeking agents.

Agents (red) follow a single rule: move one cell per timestep toward the nearest river cell (blue). When the river erodes an agent's cell, the agent is relocated to the nearest available cell. No agent has any directional preference. Under an asymmetric migration kernel, agents systematically drift along the valley in the direction of the phase lag.

Under the downstream lag (the physically realistic regime for most natural rivers), agents accumulate at the downstream end of the domain. Reversing the lag reverses the drift. The symmetric kernel produces no directional bias. Increasing the migration rate strengthens the drift; increasing agent density suppresses it through crowding.
